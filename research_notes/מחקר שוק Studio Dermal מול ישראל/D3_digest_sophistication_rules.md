## 1. Source-market sophistication

**Verdict: US = Schwartz Level 4 tipping into Level 5; UK = Level 4 with Level 5 pockets in DTC social ads** [INFERENCE from verified evidence, A7]. Every stage is documented: a simple claim, enlarged speed and duration numbers, new mechanisms (peptide "Botox in a bottle", "shale clay ... invisible shield", silicate "liquid glass"), refined execution (tint, "Easy Wear", "advanced peptide complex"), then distrust. Buyers accept that a temporary effect exists; skepticism targets duration, residue, skin safety and seller honesty:
- "doesn't last long enough or that a residue is left behind" ([MakeupAlley, result set](https://makeupalley.com/product/showreview.asp/ItemId=175907/Instantly-Ageless/Unlisted-Brand/Treatments-(Eye))) [VERIFIED]
- "Sodium silicate, also known as liquid glass" ... "highly alkaline ingredients like sodium silicate can be damaging to the skin" ([The Derm Review](https://thedermreview.com/instantly-ageless/)) [VERIFIED]. This critique targets TMP-02's own mechanism [INFERENCE].

**Implications** [INFERENCE, A7]:
- Israel: A7 puts local ad history near Level 2-3 but expects an effective Level 3-4 audience, because Israelis see the same TikTok and Instagram demos and debunks. B4 and B7 hold no Israeli sophistication evidence, so this stays unconfirmed.
- Show, do not claim: a single-take, unfiltered, timestamped demo. Say "temporary" first; the category leaders' template is appearance plus time ("visibly reducing the appearance", [KUTV](https://kutv.com/features/fresh-living/plexaderm-10-minute-challenge)) [VERIFIED].
- Pre-empt residue, cracking, wear time and gentleness only where tested; use occasion and identity framing; make trust visible (real price, no subscription, all reviews).
