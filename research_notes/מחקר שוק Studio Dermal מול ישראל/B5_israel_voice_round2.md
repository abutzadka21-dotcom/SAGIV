# B5: Israeli consumer voice, round 2: new verbatim quotes for Steps 2B, 5B and 6B (research run 4 October 2026)

**Israeli sources found: 63 distinct Israeli URLs.** 62 are confirmed Israeli by domain or Hebrew-language content; 1 is probable (an Arabic-language Super-Pharm TikTok account). 44 of the 63 are consumer-generated pages: Stips 33, Doctors.co.il forums 7, Infomed 4. The other 19 are Super-Pharm product pages (7), Israeli retail pages (5), Israeli brand Facebook pages (2), TikTok pages (4) and 1 Doctors.co.il information page. None of these URLs or quotes appear in round 1 (B2_israel_customer_voice.md).

**Bottom line on the quote target:** I collected **42 NEW Israeli verbatim strings**, each tied to its exact URL:
- 32 on-theme consumer quotes: 25 about the eye area, eye creams or eye procedures, and 7 about trusting online stores and social ads.
- 9 tangential consumer quotes.
- 1 seller's TikTok caption.

With round 1, the Israeli verbatim count rises from **20 to 62**, of which **47 are on-theme** (15 + 32). A further Russian-language title (R2-28) is probably Israeli but unconfirmed, so it is not counted. Almost all new quotes are question titles from Stips, Doctors.co.il and Infomed, not full-sentence reviews.

**Why the run stopped after 20 searches:** I made 20 searches (all in "extended" mode); one of them failed with a Threads domain error. The next 4 calls were all refused with "this session has used its web search budget (200 of 200 WebSearch calls)". That is the session-wide cap shared with the other researchers, not my 40-search allowance. As the brief requires, I stopped searching at that point. I did not use WebFetch (blocked) or any GitHub tool.

**Tagging and conventions:**
- [VERIFIED]: the text appears in a search result tied to that exact URL. Titles and captions are copied exactly as the tool returned them, typos included.
- [VERIFIED-PARAPHRASE]: the search tool's own summary of a page or result set. This is a paraphrase, not a quote, and is never shown in quotation marks as a quote.
- [INFERENCE]: my own conclusion. [GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]: background with no specific source from this run.
- Glosses in square brackets are my English translations.
- Quote IDs (R2-01 to R2-43) are global, so nothing is counted twice.
- Why the quotes are titles: the search tool returns a model-written summary, not the page text, so only page titles reliably survive word for word. On Stips (Israeli Q&A), Doctors.co.il and Infomed, the page title is the asker's own question, so it is verbatim consumer text. [GENERAL KNOWLEDGE] Stips users skew to teens and young adults. Its titles show lay vocabulary well, but they under-represent the 35+ buyer.

## Theme 1: Desires (Step 2B): make it disappear, hide it, and find "the" recommended eye cream

### Takeaway
Israeli askers want the problem to vanish. "להעלים" [make disappear] appears in 5 titles, "להוריד" [remove, literally take down] in 2 and "להעביר" [make go away] in 1. They also want a way to hide it with makeup, a peer-recommended eye cream (often "from Super-Pharm"), and, in one male asker's words, a fix that is not makeup and is urgent ("זה דחוף"). Folk remedies (tea bags, ice, cucumber, rose water) dominate the answers.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] "להעלים" (make disappear) is the default consumer verb. A temporary film-former should not echo it as a claim. Absolute "disappear" language collides with the scepticism in Theme 3 and with round 1's Israel Hayom test, and it may raise claims-compliance issues ([GENERAL KNOWLEDGE]: Israeli cosmetics advertising rules; not checked in this run). Honest copy can still meet the wish: "מותח ומחליק את המראה מתחת לעיניים תוך 2 דקות, עד שמסירים" [tightens and smooths the look under the eyes in 2 minutes, until you remove it]. That line is my copy suggestion, not a quote.
- [INFERENCE] R2-08 (a man, "without makeup", "urgent") points to a male, event-driven segment that rejects anything that reads as makeup. TMP-02's tint could be a barrier for that segment, so test it before targeting men.
- [INFERENCE] Super-Pharm is the reference point (R2-15 here; round 1's TikTok title). Israeli shoppers will judge a new product against what is on Super-Pharm shelves.
- [INFERENCE] "בנות, של איזה חברה...?" [girls, which company...?] shows that choices go through peer recommendation. Hebrew word of mouth and real-user content will matter more than brand claims.

### Gaps
- No desire posts from women aged 40+ were found. Prog (a Haredi women's forum) returned off-topic threads, and Facebook groups were not indexed.
- No consumer use of "נראית עייפה" [you look tired] was found in this round either.
