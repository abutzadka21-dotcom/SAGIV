### Inferences
1. Credibility on Israeli skincare TikTok comes from honesty and ingredient literacy, and from saying "temporary" out loud. The_dekel's caption pre-bunks exactly the kind of claim an instant tightener makes. A product literally named "Temporary Eye Tightener" can side with this voice ("it is temporary, and here is exactly what it does") instead of fighting it [INFERENCE].
2. The most accessible creator archetypes for a 40-60 audience, not yet identified by handle:
   - Medical cosmeticians (קוסמטיקאיות רפואיות) with local clienteles who book by phone or WhatsApp. Galit Babaev and Lilach Hazan show the pattern.
   - Educator-style TikTokers like @the_dekel.
   - Doctors (Acreman, Shalkowitz) have the most reach but are the least likely to endorse a temporary cosmetic, and may criticize it [INFERENCE].
3. Retailer names (Super-Pharm, Be, Terminal Beauty, Glam42, HaMashbir) act as trust anchors in Israeli beauty content. A direct-to-consumer newcomer with no shelf presence lacks that anchor and must replace it with Israeli service signals (Hebrew support, an Israeli phone or WhatsApp, local stock) [INFERENCE].
4. Israeli media treat "coupon code" influencer marketing as a familiar, slightly mocked trope. This favors creators who disclose the partnership and still critique the product (the_dekel model), or UGC that does not look sponsored [INFERENCE].
5. War-time context changed creator consumption: the nurse headline shows audiences explicitly asking for light, cheerful content during the war [INFERENCE from title].
