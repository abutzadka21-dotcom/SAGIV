## Q1. Ministry of Health cosmetics regime: notification, business register, responsible representative, Hebrew labelling, claims, advertising, personal-use import, dropshipping

### Takeaway
Israel has replaced per-product MoH licensing with an EU-style notification model under תקנות הרוקחים (תמרוקים), תשפ"ג-2023 (Kovetz Takanot 10626, 24 April 2023). A business that imports a cosmetic for sale has four duties:
- be listed in the register of cosmetics businesses (מרשם העוסקים בתמרוקים); per a consultancy, this has been a condition for importing, wholesale storage and distribution since 1.4.23
- appoint a responsible representative (נציג אחראי), registered in the MoH database of responsible representatives
- file a marketing notification before sale, with a product information file (PIF) holding safety data available to the MoH
- label in Hebrew unless the regulation says otherwise

A cosmetic is defined by purpose (cleaning, perfuming, changing appearance, protecting or maintaining), and presenting it as having a therapeutic effect without drug approval is prohibited. I found **no cosmetics-specific MoH advertising rule** and **no source settling whether dropshipping counts as personal import for MoH purposes**. Blogs say that for customs the consumer is recorded as the importer and the parcel falls under personal-import rules. By definition, though, personal import is import by an individual and not for commerce.

**Definition of a cosmetic, and the line against drug claims**
- [VERIFIED, paraphrase] Under the Pharmacists Ordinance, a cosmetic is any substance or mixture intended to come into contact with external parts of the human body, with the primary or exclusive purpose of cleaning, perfuming, changing its appearance, protecting or maintaining it, or improving body odours, and excluding pharmaceutical preparations and medical devices. Result set: [Nevo, פקודת הרוקחים [נוסח חדש], תשמ"א-1981](https://www.nevo.co.il/law_html/law01/p211_002.htm); [Hebrew Wikisource text of the Ordinance](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%A4%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%93%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%97%D7%99%D7%9D). The tool's English rendering, not the statute's Hebrew wording.
- [VERIFIED, paraphrase] A cosmetic may not be presented as having a therapeutic effect without approval as a drug; claims such as *heals*, *treats acne* or *prevents inflammation* (the tool's English examples) are treated as medical claims. Result set of search 12: most likely [avivbarishuy.co.il, "משרד הבריאות מחלקת תמרוקים"](https://avivbarishuy.co.il/%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%A8%D7%93-%D7%94%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%90%D7%95%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%97%D7%9C%D7%A7%D7%AA-%D7%AA%D7%9E%D7%A8%D7%95%D7%A7%D7%99%D7%9D/) or [Pharmaline, "סיווג לקטגוריות רגולטוריות: ההבחנה בין תרופות לתמרוקים"](https://pharmaline.co.il/articles/150137/). ## Q2. Personal import: VAT exemption threshold (75 vs 150 USD), customs exemption, 2025-2026 changes

### Takeaway
As of the latest sources found (a page stating the rule as of July 2026: "נכון ליולי 2026"), the VAT exemption on personal imports is **back at 75 USD**, calculated on the goods' value without shipping. The threshold went through two short-lived raises:
- **150 USD:** took effect on 24 December 2025; revoked by the Knesset plenum in February 2026 (59-25), most likely on 23 Feb 2026 [INFERENCE from dates].
- **130 USD:** signed less than 24 hours later and in force from midnight about 24/25 February 2026; revoked by the Knesset 59-23 on a Tuesday between March and July 2026 (exact date not returned; the tool guessed mid-May 2026).

The High Court refused to block the 150 USD order and criticised but did not cancel the 130 USD order; the Knesset, not the Court, ended both. A September 2026 search found no newer change. The customs-duty exemption ceiling is 500 USD (VAT only between 75 and 500 USD; customs too above 500 USD), per an undated guide and a 2026 blog guide. VAT is 18%. For a single 29.99 USD unit nothing changes: it is below 75 USD under every version.
