## 1. Shared values (at least 10), with examples

### Takeaway
The core value cluster is **"look like me, rested, and be seen"**:
- They want to look fresher and less tired, not younger and not "done".
- They want to be visible and represented by real, unretouched women their age.
- They want brands to talk about ageing and menopause candidly, without "anti-aging" shame.

Underneath sit trust in expertise, natural or reversible results, practical technique, value-conscious spending, heavy caring and work loads, humour, and peer community.

## 2. Visual markers (style, makeup habits, eye features)

### Takeaway
The visual identity is centred on the **eye area**: hooded upper lids, crepey "textured" lids, and under-eye darkness or puffiness treated with corrector, concealer and de-puffing. Makeup technique, not heavy coverage, is the marker:
- matte lids, primer, gel liner, a drawn "crease";
- individual lashes rather than full strips;
- semi-matte creamy bases.

In imagery the markers are **unretouched faces, real women 40s-80s, and founders of the same age demonstrating on themselves**.

## 3. Language patterns (at least 20), each with a source URL

### Takeaway
The vocabulary has four layers:
1. **Symptom words**: hooded, crepey, mature skin/eyes, tired, de-puff.
2. **Stance words**: pro-aging, age gracefully, "not to look 25 again", "art not artificiality", "never ... anti-aging".
3. **Life-stage words**: midlife, Gen X, over 40/50, perimenopause/menopause, sandwich generation, second act, mother of the bride.
4. **Contempt words** for the opposite: tweakment face, pillow face, overfilled, Sephora kids.

UK usage adds "tweakments" and "grown up beauty". US usage leans on "over 50", "Gen X" and AARP framing.

## 9. Belonging signals, opposing identities, and how the identity changed in the last 5 years

### Takeaway
**Belonging is signalled by:**
- age-community hashtags and self-labels: #over40makeup, #matureskin, "midlife", "Gen X", "grown up";
- open menopause talk and products ("menopause-friendly");
- following same-age founders and doctors;
- showing real, unretouched faces;
- humour.

**Opposing identities:**
- "Sephora kids" (Gen Alpha using anti-aging skincare);
- the overfilled "pillow face" / "tweakment face";
- "anti-aging" and "reverse" marketing;
- stereotyped, retouched advertising;
- society's "decline" narrative.

**Change since 2021:** menopause went mainstream in the UK (Davina, May 2021, then the HRT surge, Boots zones, No7 range). Midlife beauty moved onto TikTok with founder-educators (Bobbi Brown, Jan 2022). Retailers repositioned to 50+ (QVC 2024). Pro-aging manifestos (Stripes 2025) and a backlash against overfilled faces and toward dissolving filler followed. Doctor-creators with multi-million followings appeared (Haver, 2026).
