# B2: Israeli customer voice on under-eye bags, puffiness, dark circles, eye creams, instant-effect products and eye-area procedures (Hebrew sources, research run 4 October 2026)

**Israeli sources found: 91 distinct Israeli URLs cited in these notes** (breakdown in Key Question 7; 100 URLs in total, of which 8 il.iherb.com pages and 1 amazon.com page are not counted as Israeli). Of these 91, 35 are consumer-generated pages (Tapuz, FXP, Doctors.co.il forums, an Israeli review blogger, a Hebrew TikTok discover page, an Israeli skincare blog, Zap store and product pages). The others are Israeli journalism, retail, brand and clinic pages. il.iherb.com pages are counted separately because their reviews are global, not Israeli.

**Bottom line on the quote target:** I did **not** reach the minimum of 50 verbatim Israeli quotes. I found **20 verbatim Hebrew consumer-written strings** (15 on topic and 5 tangential or with an unconfirmed topic). Almost all of them are forum thread titles, not full posts. On top of those I have 4 Hebrew paraphrases of Israeli pages (plus 1 of a US Amazon listing) and 17 English paraphrases of Israeli consumer or reviewer content, plus 53 verbatim Hebrew headlines and product or clinic titles from Israeli pages that show market language but are not consumer voice. I found **0 Arabic or Russian items** (none were searched). The reasons are below and in Key Question 7.

**Why the target was missed (read before using these notes):**
1. The WebSearch budget for the whole session (200 calls, shared with the other researchers) ran out after **27 executed queries by this researcher**. Two more queries were refused with "this session has used its web search budget (200 of 200 WebSearch calls)".
2. WebFetch is blocked. My single permitted attempt, on the Israel Hayom consumer test at https://www.israelhayom.co.il/business/consumers/article/19287112, returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`. I made no further WebFetch attempts and used no other way around the block.
3. The search tool returns a summary written by a model, not the raw page text. Verbatim consumer sentences rarely survive it. What does survive verbatim is mainly titles (thread titles, headlines, product names) and a few Hebrew lines.
4. `site:shop.super-pharm.co.il` returned no Super-Pharm pages at all. Facebook groups are closed or not indexed. No TikTok or YouTube comments were returned.

**Tagging used throughout:**

- [INFERENCE]: my own conclusion.

- Glosses in square brackets are my English translations.

## Key Question 1: What words do Israelis use for the problem, and how do novice and expert terms differ?

### Takeaway
Lay Israelis name the problem bluntly, by colour and shape: "שחור מתחת לעיניים", "אזור שחור", "עיגולים שחורים", "שקיות", "נפיחות", "קמטים מתחת לעיניים". Brands, clinics and enthusiasts use "כהויות", "עיגולים כהים", "שקעים / מילוי שקעים", "קמטוטים", "העור הדקיק", "מיצוק", "מראה רענן". A hybrid lay term, "שקיות שחורות" [black bags], merges bags and dark circles. In this run I could verify "tired look" wording only in editorial or clinic copy, not in consumer posts.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Lay Israelis describe the colour ("שחור") and the object ("שקיות") rather than the mechanism. Hebrew ads for a TMP-02-type product should open with "שקיות", "נפיחות", "שחור / עיגולים שחורים מתחת לעיניים" and "קמטוטים", and keep jargon such as "כהויות" or "שקעים" for the details block.
- [INFERENCE] No consumer-level Hebrew word for "crepey" appeared. The closest verified pieces are "קמטוטים", "העור הדקיק סביב העיניים" and "עור גס מתחת לעיניים". A natural Hebrew rendering of "crepey texture" would describe the look (for example "עור דק ומקומט מתחת לעיניים"). That is a copy suggestion, not a quote.
- [INFERENCE] Because "שקיות שחורות" merges two problems, Israeli buyers may expect one product to fix both puffiness and darkness. A tinted film-former can credibly claim to "cover and tighten" (tint plus film), but it should not imply that it removes pigmentation.
- [INFERENCE] Novice signals are folk remedies (cucumbers, tea bags, hemorrhoid ointment) and "מה שמים?" [what do you put on?] questions. Expert signals are ingredient and procedure words ("חומצה היאלורונית", "שקעים", "בוטוקס בצידי העיניים", "פריימר", "קפאין").

### Gaps
- I could not verify consumer use of "מבט עייף", "עיניים עייפות", "נראית עייפה כל הזמן" or "עור רפוי מתחת לעיניים". "Tired look" appeared only in an editorial or clinic paraphrase (KQ2 item 16).
- No Arabic or Russian Israeli sources were searched because the budget ran out.

## Key Question 2: Desires and problems (Step 2B). What do Israeli consumers want, and what bothers them?

### Takeaway
The thin evidence points to practical, low-effort aims. Consumers want to prevent puffiness, cool and de-puff at home, conceal under makeup (primer plus concealer), and get peer recommendations ("מה ממולץ"). Problems are named as a visible black area, bags, under-eye wrinkles, bags felt to be hereditary, and complications after aesthetic treatments. An Israeli reviewer valued an eye cream's short-term blur and primer effect even though it did nothing for puffiness. That is exactly the benefit TMP-02 offers.
