## Key Question 1: The indirect competitor categories: price, time to result, effort, risks, category language, advantages and disadvantages against a temporary tightener, and share trend

### Takeaway
At least 11 categories compete for the same desire. They fall into three groups:
- **(a) Cheap, instant but short-lived fixes:** cooling tools, hydrogel patches, concealer, and hacks such as Preparation H.
- **(b) Slow, cumulative home routes:** eye creams and serums, LED, microcurrent and RF devices, and lifestyle changes.
- **(c) Expensive needle, energy or scalpel routes:** Botox, tear trough filler, Morpheus8 and polynucleotides, and lower blepharoplasty.

TMP-02 sits in the gap between (a) and (c). It works as instantly as concealer but promises a change in texture and puffiness, at about 1/9 to 1/20 of a crow's-feet Botox session and about 1/15 to 1/35 of a UK tear trough filler [INFERENCE]. The clearest evidence of shifting share:
- Global hyaluronic acid (filler) procedures fell **25% in 2025** (ISAPS, released 29 Sept 2026).
- **Eyelid surgery became the world's #1 surgical procedure** in 2024 and again in 2025 (ISAPS).
- Search interest in **hydrogel under-eye patches** rose +5200% (Exploding Topics).
- The Preparation H "butt cream" hack keeps going viral and is explicitly framed as a cheap substitute for filler.

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] **TMP-02's real competitive set is "instant and cheap" (concealer, patches, ice tools, hacks), not "tweakments".** Its price ($29.99) matches a prestige concealer ($27.15 / £21.75) and is 9x to 270x below clinical routes. The strongest wedge is a pain point each instant rival leaves open:
  - concealer: creasing and caking;
  - cold tools: "only temporary" once the skin warms;
  - patches: the effect fades by midday and they cannot be worn under makeup;
  - Preparation H: not safe near the eyes.
- [INFERENCE] **The decline of filler is an opportunity.** Global HA procedures fell 25% in 2025 while toxin stayed #1 and surgery grew. Some of the people who would once have bought under-eye filler are now filler-averse, worried about Tyndall, migration or occlusion, or cutting back on cost. A "no needles, no regret, wash it off tonight" message speaks directly to them. Caveat: the ISAPS decline is global and covers all HA filler, not tear troughs specifically, and survey methods change from year to year.
- [INFERENCE] **Surgery is the "definitive" end and it is growing**, especially among older patients (ASPS 66+) and men (BAAPS, ISAPS). TMP-02 can be positioned as a bridge: "for the days before you decide, or instead of deciding".
- [INFERENCE] **Botox is a complement, not a substitute.** It treats crow's-feet lines, while TMP-02's claims centre on puffiness and crepey texture under the eye. Many Botox users still have bags, so they are a cross-sell audience.
- [INFERENCE] **Devices and creams sell patience; TMP-02 sells proof.** Omnilux's own protocol asks for 4-6 weeks of 10-minute sessions 3-5 times a week. A tightener can demonstrate itself in a 60-second video, which explains why it scales on Meta ads.
- [INFERENCE] The Preparation H story is a ready-made content hook ("the butt-cream hack, done safely"). Its age (2018 to TikTok) shows durable consumer appetite for a cheap "tightening" topical.

## Key Question 4: The typical sequence people try before buying an instant tightener, and their emotional relationship with each alternative

### Takeaway
No source in this session documents the actual sequence (no survey or article found; the search budget ran out). The evidence that was found supports a hypothesised **escalation ladder** that runs from free and lifestyle fixes, to cheap tools and hacks, to creams and concealer, to devices, and finally to clinic treatments and surgery. Each step trades more money, risk and commitment for more certainty. The instant tightener fits in two places: as the "upgrade" from concealer, hacks and cold tools when their effect fades, and as the "step back" for people who considered or dropped needles or surgery. The emotional map runs from hope and impatience (creams, devices), through frustration (concealer creasing) and thrill mixed with guilt (hacks), to fear and regret (filler) and a sense of high stakes (surgery).

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] **Hypothesised escalation ladder** (to test with Israeli consumers and with UK/US reviews). Each step is roughly 3-10x more expensive or committed than the one before:
  1. **Free fixes:** sleep, less salt and alcohol, allergy control, cold water, cold spoons, chilled tea bags. Emotion: self-blame ("I should sleep more"), a sense of control, quick disappointment.
  2. **Cheap tools and treats:** ice roller or globes, hydrogel patches before events. Emotion: a soothing self-care ritual and pleasure; mild disappointment when the effect fades by mid-morning.
  3. **Eye cream or serum, often several in turn:** caffeine, retinol, peptides. Emotion: hope, then scepticism ("eye creams don't work"); sunk-cost habit.
  4. **Concealer and colour corrector:** daily cover-up. Emotion: dependence plus frustration (creasing, cakiness, "looks worse by 3 pm").
  5. **Viral hacks:** Preparation H, eyelid tape. Emotion: the thrill of an insider secret, guilt and fear ("experts warn it's dangerous").
  6. **Home devices:** LED, microcurrent. Emotion: an investment made in hope, then impatience; guilt when the device goes unused.
  7. **Instant tightener (TMP-02 category):** event or daily use. Emotion: delight at a visible "before/after" in minutes; then possible annoyance at drying time, residue or the fact that it does not last.
  8. **Tweakments:** Botox for crow's feet; tear trough filler, polynucleotides, Morpheus8. Emotion: anxiety (needles, "overdone" looks, blue tint), relief when done well, regret when not.
  9. **Lower blepharoplasty:** Emotion: high stakes and finality ("finally fixed"), fear of surgery and downtime.
- [INFERENCE] **Where TMP-02 enters.** Most often between steps 4 and 6, when concealer and patches have disappointed. It also enters from above, when someone has priced or feared steps 8-9, or is between Botox or filler appointments. Ad messaging should therefore cover both "better than concealer, safer than the hack" and "no needles, no downtime".
